Key findings

  • Silent authentication is worth 23 points of conversion. Sessions confirmed by the carrier book at 71%; sessions that fall back to a one-time code book at 48%.
  • 31% of sessions fall back to a one-time code. The OTP path should stay a single screen with autofill enabled so the user is never sent out of the flow to retrieve a code manually.
  • Returning users convert best at 78%. Device recognition removes the identity step entirely on repeat visits. Binding the device on first contact compounds, and this cohort is 17% of volume today.
  • Scheduling is the largest single leak at 19.4%. These users were already verified and already had coverage returned, so the loss is driven by availability rather than by verification.
  • 28% of coverage lookups return nothing, and those sessions collect no copay. At current volume that is roughly $25,000 per month in payment left uncollected at booking. A card-capture fallback should be added before the payment step rather than after the visit.
  • Intake abandonment fell from 34.8% to 7.1%. The residual is concentrated in sessions where both identity paths failed and the user was routed to manual entry, for example someone on a prepaid line the carrier could not match, who is then asked to type name, address, date of birth and insurance details by hand.

Biggest leak: choosing a time

19.4% of users who confirmed their details never picked a time slot. Every one of them had already been verified and already had coverage returned, so nothing about identity or insurance caused the drop. They saw one day of availability and left. Worth testing a three-day slot window instead of one, or surfacing the next opening on the clinician's profile.
